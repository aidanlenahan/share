## Answer Key

**1. B.** Oxidation numbers come from heterolytic cleavage, giving both bonding electrons to the more electronegative atom (a 100% ionic assumption). Homolytic cleavage (A) gives formal charges.

**2. A.** Formal charge on H is 0 (equal sharing), oxidation number is +1 (100% ionic), and the calculated charge is intermediate at +0.18.

**3. D.** Cl (3.16) is more electronegative than N (3.04), so Cl is −1. N = 0 − 3(−1) = +3.

**4. D.** O = −2. S = charge − Σ(known) = −2 − 4(−2) = −2 + 8 = +6.

**5. D.** Because of the O–O bond, O is not −2. With H = +1: 2(+1) + 2x = 0, so x = −1.

**6. C.** Mn = −1 − 4(−2) = −1 + 8 = +7.

**7. A.** 2x + 7(−2) = −2, so 2x = +12 and x = +6.

**8. D.** H bonded to Si is assigned −1 (one of the exceptions). Si = 0 − 4(−1) = +4.

**9. C.** In OF₂, F is always −1, so O = 0 − 2(−1) = +2. Oxygen is −2 in H₂O and Na₂O and −1 in H₂O₂.

**10. B.** Cl = −1, so Fe = 0 − 3(−1) = +3, written as the Roman numeral III: iron(III) chloride.

**11. C.** SN = CN + LP = x + e.

**12. B.** N bonds to 3 H atoms (CN = 3) and has one lone pair (LP = 1), so SN = 4 and it is AX₃E.

**13. D.** H₂O is AX₂E₂: its electron geometry is tetrahedral, but its molecular geometry is bent. The other three have no lone pairs, so the molecular shape matches the idealized geometry.

**14. A.** CH₄ is 109.5°, NH₃ is 107° and H₂O is 104.5°. Each added lone pair compresses the H–A–H angle.

**15. A.** A lone pair is physically larger than a bond pair, so lone pair/bond pair repulsions are greater and push the bonds closer together.

**16. C.** SN = 7 gives a pentagonal bipyramid, with 72° and 90° angles. Octahedral is SN = 6 and trigonal bipyramidal is SN = 5.

**17. B.** An equatorial lone pair has only two 90° interactions, compared with three if it were axial. 90° interactions dominate because the domains are closer together, so the result is a see-saw shape.

**18. C.** ClF₃ is AX₃E₂ (SN = 5) with both lone pairs equatorial, which gives a T-shape.

**19. A.** All three lone pairs occupy the equatorial plane, leaving the two F atoms axial at 180°, so the molecule is linear.

**20. B.** XeF₄ is AX₄E₂. Lone pair–lone pair repulsion is the strongest, so the lone pairs sit 180° apart and the four F atoms form a square plane.

**21. A.** A double bond counts as one domain. CN = 3 and LP = 0, so SN = 3, AX₃, trigonal planar.

**22. C.** In every Lewis structure CN = 4 and LP = 0, so SN = 4 and the ion is AX₄ tetrahedral. VSEPR geometry does not depend on the Lewis structure chosen.

**23. B.** S has 2 bonded O atoms and 1 lone pair, so SN = 3 and it is AX₂E. The electron geometry is trigonal planar and the molecular shape is bent. This holds for both the 10-electron and the 8-electron Lewis structures.

**24. B.** H₂S is AX₂E₂ (SN = 4), so VSEPR predicts about 109.5° (somewhat less), but the actual angle is 92°. This is a cited example of VSEPR's limitations, where MO theory is more sound.

**25. D.** BF₃ is trigonal planar (AX₃), so its three polar B–F bond dipoles cancel. H₂O, NH₃ and CH₃Cl are polar.

**26. D.** SF₄ is see-saw (AX₄E), so its bond dipoles do not cancel. CO₂ (linear), CCl₄ (tetrahedral) and SF₆ (octahedral) are symmetric and nonpolar.

**27. B.** More than 8,000 of the more than 10,000 tons produced each year are used as a gaseous dielectric in high-voltage circuit breakers. Nike stopped using SF₆ in 2006 because it is a potent greenhouse gas.

**28. C.** ν = c/λ = (3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹)/(500 × 10⁻⁹ m) = 6.0 × 10¹⁴ s⁻¹.

**29. C.** λ = c/ν = (3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹)/(1.00 × 10⁸ s⁻¹) = 3.00 m, which is in the radio range of about 1–10⁴ m.

**30. B.** Red light is at the long-wavelength (about 750 nm), low-frequency end of the visible region. All light travels at c in a vacuum, and lower frequency means lower photon energy.

**31. A.** In increasing wavelength the order is gamma < X-ray < UV < visible < IR < microwave (about 10⁻² m) < radio.

**32. D.** E = hc/λ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s)(3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹)/(600 × 10⁻⁹ m) = 3.32 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.

**33. C.** E = (3.315 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)(6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹) = 1.996 × 10⁵ J/mol ≈ 200 kJ/mol.

**34. C.** Allowed energies are ε = nhν, and hν = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴)(1.0 × 10¹³) = 6.63 × 10⁻²¹ J. For n = 3, ε = 1.99 × 10⁻²⁰ J ≈ 2.0 × 10⁻²⁰ J. The other choices are not integer multiples of 6.63 × 10⁻²¹ J (they correspond to n ≈ 1.5, 0.5 and 0.75).

**35. D.** By Stefan's law I ∝ T⁴, so doubling T gives 2⁴ = 16 times the intensity.

**36. A.** T·λmax is constant, so λmax(1000 K) = (800 K)(3.6 µm)/(1000 K) = 2.88 µm ≈ 2.9 µm. The hotter body peaks at a shorter wavelength.

**37. C.** λmax = (2.90 × 10⁻³ m K)/(5800 K) = 5.0 × 10⁻⁷ m = 500 nm, which is in the visible region (400–750 nm).

**38. B.** Classical (Rayleigh–Jeans) theory predicted that intensity keeps rising as frequency increases, so any warm object would emit intense UV and even X-rays. Experiment instead shows a maximum.

**39. C.** Planck proposed ε = nhν. A cool body does not have enough energy to supply the large quantum hν that a high-frequency oscillator needs, so UV emission is suppressed. If h were zero, the catastrophe would return.

**40. D.** Intensity reflects the number of energy packets. The energy of each packet, E = hν = hc/λ, depends only on frequency, so it is unchanged.

**41. B.** Classically, an orbiting electron is an accelerating charge, so it should radiate energy and spiral into the nucleus (the "death spiral").

**42. C.** Wedgwood's scale was 550 °C dark red, 750 °C cherry red, 900 °C orange, 1000 °C yellow and 1200 °C white.
