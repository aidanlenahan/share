## Answer Key

**1. D.** Intensity is the number of photons. More photons eject more electrons, but each photon still has the same energy hν, so KE_max = hν − Φ does not change.

**2. A.** One photon gives its energy to one electron. If hν < Φ, no electron is ejected no matter how many photons arrive. Violet photons have higher ν, so hν > Φ and ejection is immediate.

**3. C.** KE = hν − Φ, so KE grows linearly with ν. Intensity and exposure time only change the number of photons, and a longer wavelength means a lower frequency and a smaller KE.

**4. D.** E = hc/λ = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s)(3.00 × 10⁸ m/s)/(500 × 10⁻⁹ m) = 3.98 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.

**5. A.** Φ = hν₀ = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s)(5.44 × 10¹⁴ s⁻¹) = 3.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.

**6. B.** E_photon = hc/λ = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴)(3.00 × 10⁸)/(400 × 10⁻⁹) = 4.9695 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Φ = hν₀ = 3.6045 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. KE = hν − Φ = 4.9695 × 10⁻¹⁹ − 3.6045 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 1.36 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. (4.97 × 10⁻¹⁹ J is the photon energy alone; it forgets to subtract Φ.)

**7. D.** At threshold, hc/λ₀ = Φ, so λ₀ = hc/Φ = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴)(3.00 × 10⁸)/(3.00 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 6.63 × 10⁻⁷ m = 663 nm. Longer wavelengths have photons with less energy than Φ.

**8. A.** E_photon = hc/λ = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴)(3.00 × 10⁸)/(300 × 10⁻⁹) = 6.626 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Φ = hν − KE = 6.626 × 10⁻¹⁹ − 2.00 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 4.63 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.

**9. C.** ν₀ = Φ/h = (4.00 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)/(6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s) = 6.04 × 10¹⁴ s⁻¹ = 6.04 × 10¹⁴ Hz.

**10. C.** KE = hν − Φ is a straight line with slope h for every metal. The x-intercept (ν₀) and the extrapolated y-intercept (−Φ) depend on the metal.

**11. D.** Classical physics says a charge moving in a circular path radiates energy. The electron would lose energy and spiral into the nucleus (the "death spiral"), so classical atoms would be unstable.

**12. A.** A gas discharge tube (low-pressure gas, high voltage) gives a line (emission) spectrum: bright lines only at wavelengths characteristic of the element. A continuous spectrum comes from sources such as light bulbs and stars.

**13. B.** This is an absorption spectrum. The atoms absorb only photons that match their energy gaps, so those wavelengths appear as black lines on the continuous spectrum.

**14. D.** Each element has its own set of allowed energy levels, so the number, colors and positions of its lines are a unique signature of that element.

**15. B.** Balmer lines (656, 486, 434, 410 nm) are visible and come from transitions to n₁ = 2. Transitions to n = 1 are the ultraviolet Lyman series; transitions to n = 3 are in the infrared.

**16. B.** 1/λ = R_H(1/2² − 1/3²) = (1.097 × 10⁷)(0.25 − 0.1111) = (1.097 × 10⁷)(0.13889) = 1.524 × 10⁶ m⁻¹, so λ = 6.56 × 10⁻⁷ m = 656 nm (the red Balmer line).

**17. A.** 1/λ = (1.097 × 10⁷)(1/4 − 1/16) = (1.097 × 10⁷)(0.1875) = 2.057 × 10⁶ m⁻¹, so λ = 486 nm. Balmer lines end on n = 2: 656 (3→2), 486 (4→2), 434 (5→2), 410 (6→2).

**18. D.** ΔE = R_E(1/1² − 1/2²) = (2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸)(0.75) = 1.635 × 10⁻¹⁸ J. λ = hc/ΔE = (1.988 × 10⁻²⁵)/(1.635 × 10⁻¹⁸) = 1.22 × 10⁻⁷ m = 122 nm. Transitions to n = 1 are the Lyman series, in the ultraviolet.

**19. C.** 1/λ = R_H(1/1² − 1/3²) = (1.097 × 10⁷)(0.8889) = 9.751 × 10⁶ m⁻¹, so λ = 1.03 × 10⁻⁷ m = 103 nm (Lyman series, ultraviolet).

**20. A.** 1/λ = R_H(1/2² − 1/∞²) = (1.097 × 10⁷)(0.25) = 2.743 × 10⁶ m⁻¹, so λ = 3.65 × 10⁻⁷ m = 365 nm. This is the highest-energy Balmer transition.

**21. C.** 1/λ = R_H(1/3² − 1/4²) = (1.097 × 10⁷)(0.11111 − 0.0625) = (1.097 × 10⁷)(0.048611) = 5.333 × 10⁵ m⁻¹, so λ = 1.875 × 10⁻⁶ m = 1875 nm. Transitions ending on n = 3 are in the infrared.

**22. D.** R_E = hcR_H = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s)(3.00 × 10⁸ m/s)(1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹) = 2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸ J (= 13.6 eV).

**23. B.** Bohr assumed circular orbits described by classical physics, but only orbits with quantized angular momentum mₑvr = n(h/2π) = nħ are allowed, and no energy is emitted in them. Energy changes only when the electron passes between orbits.

**24. B.** Eₙ = −R_E/n². As n grows, 1/n² changes less and less, so the levels converge toward E = 0 at n = ∞.

**25. C.** ΔE = R_E(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²). For 2→1 the factor is 1 − 1/4 = 0.75; for 3→2 it is 0.139; for 4→3, 0.0486; for 6→5, 0.0122. The gaps shrink at higher n, so 2→1 is largest.

**26. D.** Light is absorbed when an electron jumps from a lower orbit to a higher orbit (2 → 4). The other three are jumps from higher to lower orbits, which emit photons.

**27. C.** rₙ = n²a₀ = 3² × 0.529 Å = 4.76 Å.

**28. B.** For a hydrogen-like ion, rₙ = n²a₀/Z = (2²)(0.529 Å)/2 = 1.06 Å.

**29. A.** E₃ = −R_E/3² = −(2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸)/9 = −2.42 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Ionization takes the electron to E = 0 (n = ∞), so it requires 2.42 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.

**30. A.** ΔE = R_E(1/2² − 1/4²) = (2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸)(0.1875) = 4.0875 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. ν = ΔE/h = (4.0875 × 10⁻¹⁹)/(6.626 × 10⁻³⁴) = 6.17 × 10¹⁴ Hz.

**31. C.** Ionization from n = 1 requires ΔE = R_E = 2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸ J. λ = hc/ΔE = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴)(3.00 × 10⁸)/(2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸) = 9.12 × 10⁻⁸ m = 91.2 nm (ultraviolet).

**32. A.** ΔE(1→2) = (2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸)(1 − 1/4) = 1.635 × 10⁻¹⁸ J. ΔE(1→3) = (2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸)(1 − 1/9) = 1.94 × 10⁻¹⁸ J. Ionization needs 2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸ J. The photon energy, 1.80 × 10⁻¹⁸ J, matches none of these, so by the resonance condition it is not absorbed.

**33. D.** Hydrogen-like ions have exactly one electron. Li²⁺ (Z = 3) has 3 − 2 = 1 electron. Li⁺ and He have two electrons, and H⁻ has two electrons.

**34. B.** Eₙ = −R_E Z²/n². For He⁺, Z = 2 and n = 1, so E₁ = −(2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸)(4)/1 = −8.72 × 10⁻¹⁸ J. Removing the electron (to E = 0) takes 8.72 × 10⁻¹⁸ J.

**35. C.** E₁ = −R_E Z²/n² = −13.6 eV × 3²/1² = −13.6 × 9 = −122 eV.

**36. A.** ΔE = R_E Z²(1/2² − 1/3²) = (2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸)(4)(0.13889) = 1.211 × 10⁻¹⁸ J. λ = hc/ΔE = (1.988 × 10⁻²⁵)/(1.211 × 10⁻¹⁸) = 1.64 × 10⁻⁷ m = 164 nm. Because of the Z² factor, this is 1/4 of hydrogen's 656 nm.

**37. B.** The Bohr model successfully explained the hydrogen line spectrum (it reproduces the Rydberg equation). The other three statements are the shortcomings listed in the lecture.
