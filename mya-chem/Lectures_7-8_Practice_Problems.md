# Lectures 7–8 Practice Problems: Lewis Structures, Formal Charge, Resonance and the Octet Rule

Choose the single best answer for each question. Answers and explanations are in the Answer Key at the end.

**Formulas used in these lectures**

- Formal charge = group valence − (number of lone pair electrons) − (number of bonds)
- Electron count on A ("counting up") = valence electrons of neutral A + number of bonds − formal charge
- Nonbonding electrons on A ("counting down") = valence electrons of neutral A − number of bonds − formal charge
- A double bond counts as 2 bonds and a triple bond as 3 bonds.

## Answer Key

**1. C.** Sulfur is in old Group VI (new Group 16), and the number of valence electrons equals the old group number (last digit of the new group number), so 6.

**2. B.** Core electrons correspond to the previous noble gas, [Ne] = 10. Equivalently 17 total − 7 valence = 10.

**3. D.** S is in the same group as O, so both have 6 valence electrons (valence isoelectronic).

**4. C.** Hydrogen follows the duet rule and transition elements follow the 18 electron rule.

**5. A.** Langmuir introduced the terms "covalent bond" and "octet theory" and popularized Lewis' concepts.

**6. D.** C forms 4 bonds (0 LP), N 3 bonds (1 LP), O 2 bonds (2 LP), F 1 bond (3 LP). Lone pairs emerge as you move right from carbon.

**7. B.** B has 3 valence electrons, forms 3 bonds, and has 3 + 3 = 6 electrons (a sextet) with an empty orbital. BH3 is highly reactive and reacts to gain an octet, not to form BH5.

**8. A.** In a dative bond a single atom (the donor) supplies both electrons, e.g. the N lone pair in H3N–BH3.

**9. C.** N: 5 − 0 − 4 = +1. B: 3 − 0 − 4 = −1.

**10. B.** A Lewis acid accepts an electron pair. BH3 accepts the lone pair donated by NH3 (the Lewis base).

**11. D.** Formal charge = 6 − 2 (one lone pair) − 3 bonds = +1.

**12. A.** C has 1 lone pair (2 electrons) and 3 bonds: 4 − 2 − 3 = −1. (O: 6 − 2 − 3 = +1.)

**13. B.** The lecture notes that carbon is the negative end of the CO dipole even though O is more electronegative. CO has formal charges (C⁻, O⁺) and is not cleaved by H2 the way N2 is.

**14. D.** Formal charges are not real charges and do not describe the electron distribution. They are bookkeeping, but they still must be shown in Lewis structures.

**15. C.** 3 + 4 − (−1) = 8.

**16. B.** Nonbonding electrons = 6 − 3 − (+1) = 2, which is one lone pair.

**17. A.** 5 − 4 (two lone pairs) − 2 bonds = −1. Check: electron count = 5 + 2 − (−1) = 8.

**18. D.** From the hydrides NH3, H2O and HCl, the common valences are N 3, O 2 and Cl 1.

**19. B.** C: 4 − 2 − 3 = −1. N: 5 − 2 − 3 = 0. Sum = −1, which matches the ion's charge.

**20. C.** Elements left of C (e.g. B in BH3) have empty orbitals. Elements right of C have filled orbitals, i.e. lone pairs.

**21. B.** 6 − 2 − 3 = +1. This is the O⁺ motif, isoelectronic with neutral N.

**22. C.** N⁺ with 4 bonds is isoelectronic with neutral C with 4 bonds, and so is B⁻ with 4 bonds. Choices A and D (3 bonds + 1 lone pair) match the C⁻/N/O⁺ series, and B matches N⁻/O, not four-bonded C. (Every one of these motifs has an octet, so "same number of bonds and lone pairs" is what distinguishes them.)

**23. D.** Electron count = 5 + 6 bonds (three double bonds) − (−1) = 12. Moving pairs to oxygen gives 10 and then 8.

**24. A.** Cl count = 7 + 8 bonds (four double bonds) − (−1) = 16.

**25. C.** Cl: 7 − 0 − 4 = +3. Each O: 6 − 6 − 1 = −1. Total = +3 + 4(−1) = −1. Check: Cl count = 7 + 4 − 3 = 8.

**26. B.** O3 is a hybrid of two equivalent structures, each with one single and one double O–O bond, so each bond order = (1 + 2)/2 = 1.5.

**27. C.** Resonance is not oscillation, and the ↔ arrow is not an equilibrium arrow. The real molecule is an average (like green from blue and yellow), and resonance structures differ only in the positions of electrons.

**28. D.** HCN and HNC have different atom connectivity (the nuclei are in different positions), so they are different molecules, not resonance structures.

**29. A.** The central N has 5 bonds (3 to the terminal N and 2 to O): 5 + 5 − 0 = 10. The terminal atoms automatically have 8.

**30. D.** Central N has 4 bonds and no lone pairs: 5 − 0 − 4 = +1. O⁻: 6 − 6 − 1 = −1. Terminal N: 5 − 2 − 3 = 0.

**31. A.** Taking one P–O bond back to oxygen gives P⁺ (5 − 0 − 4 = +1, count 5 + 4 − 1 = 8) and O⁻. The d-orbital explanation is wrong.

**32. B.** Moving a bond pair onto X as a lone pair lowers Y's count by 2. X's count stays the same because the pair stays in X's valence shell.

**33. C.** Total = 4 + 6 + 6 = 16, and two bonds use 4 electrons, so 16 − 4 = 12 remain (6 pairs).

**34. B.** N (5) + O (6) = 11 valence electrons. With an odd count, at least one atom must lack an octet.

**35. B.** O=N=O puts 9 electrons on N (bad). O=N⁺–O⁻ gives N 7 electrons and both O atoms 8, which is the better structure.

**36. B.** F⁺ forms 2 bonds with 2 LP: 7 − 4 − 2 = +1. B: 3 − 0 − 4 = −1. The lecture states that a positive formal charge on F is acceptable because it is not a real charge.

**37. C.** The calculated charges are N ≈ −0.74 and each H ≈ +0.44 (−0.74 + 4 × 0.44 = +1.02 ≈ +1). N is negative because it is more electronegative than H.

**38. D.** 6 valence electrons + 6 bonds = 12 apparent electrons. This is the "valence expansion" that calculations show is not real.

**39. B.** Calculations show there is no real valence shell expansion. The 3d orbitals are too high in energy to use, and the octet is kept, but the bonding cannot be described with only 2-center-2-electron bonds.

**40. A.** Each structure has one Xe–F bond and one "no-bond" Xe⁺ F⁻ interaction. The average is (1 + 0)/2 = 0.5 per Xe–F bond, and Xe has 8 electrons in each structure.
