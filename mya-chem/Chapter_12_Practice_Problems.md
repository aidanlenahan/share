## Answer Key

**1. A.** ν = c/λ = (2.9979 × 10⁸ m/s)/(6.50 × 10⁻⁷ m) = 4.61 × 10¹⁴ s⁻¹. The wavelength must be converted from nm to m before dividing.

**2. C.** λ = c/ν = (2.9979 × 10⁸ m/s)/(9.85 × 10⁷ s⁻¹) = 3.04 m.

**3. C.** Frequency and wavelength are inversely related (λν = c). Gamma rays have the shortest wavelengths, so they have the highest frequency.

**4. C.** In order of increasing wavelength: gamma rays, X rays, ultraviolet, visible, infrared, microwaves, radio waves.

**5. A.** All EM radiation travels at c in a vacuum, with mutually perpendicular oscillating electric and magnetic fields; λ and ν are inversely related (λν = c).

**6. C.** Frequency is the number of cycles passing a point per second; since 'cycles' is understood, 1 Hz = 1 s⁻¹.

**7. B.** E = hc/λ = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s)(2.9979 × 10⁸ m/s)/(5.20 × 10⁻⁷ m) = 3.82 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.

**8. C.** Per photon: E = hc/λ = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴)(2.9979 × 10⁸)/(3.00 × 10⁻⁷ m) = 6.62 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Per mole: (6.62 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)(6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹) = 3.99 × 10⁵ J/mol = 399 kJ/mol.

**9. D.** λ = hc/E = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s)(2.9979 × 10⁸ m/s)/(3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ J) = 6.21 × 10⁻⁷ m = 621 nm.

**10. B.** E = hν, so ν = E/h = (5.00 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)/(6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s) = 7.55 × 10¹⁴ s⁻¹.

**11. B.** Planck postulated that energy changes occur only in integer multiples (n = 1, 2, 3, …) of hν; each packet hν is a quantum.

**12. A.** Classical physics, assuming matter can absorb or emit any amount of energy, predicted a blackbody profile with no maximum that goes to infinite intensity at short wavelengths. Planck's quantization fixed this.

**13. D.** The intensity maximum shifts to shorter wavelengths at higher temperatures, matching the observed change from a reddish to a white glow.

**14. D.** Each photon has energy hν < hν₀ = E₀, so no single photon can remove an electron. Intensity only changes the number of photons, not their energy.

**15. A.** Intensity measures the number of photons. More photons release more electrons, but each electron's KE = hν − hν₀ depends only on frequency.

**16. B.** E₀ = hν₀, so ν₀ = E₀/h = (7.21 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)/(6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s) = 1.09 × 10¹⁵ s⁻¹.

**17. B.** E_photon = hc/λ = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴)(2.9979 × 10⁸)/(2.50 × 10⁻⁷ m) = 7.95 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. KE = hν − hν₀ = 7.95 × 10⁻¹⁹ J − 3.70 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 4.25 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.

**18. D.** λ = h/(mv) = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ kg·m²/s)/[(9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg)(2.5 × 10⁶ m/s)] = 2.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ m. (1 J = 1 kg·m²/s²)

**19. A.** λ = h/(mv) = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ kg·m²/s)/[(0.145 kg)(40.0 m/s)] = 1.14 × 10⁻³⁴ m. This is far too small to observe, so the ball behaves as a particle.

**20. A.** λ = h/(mv), so v = h/(mλ) = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ kg·m²/s)/[(9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg)(1.00 × 10⁻¹⁰ m)] = 7.27 × 10⁶ m/s.

**21. D.** λ = h/(mv) = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ kg·m²/s)/[(1.675 × 10⁻²⁷ kg)(1.00 × 10³ m/s)] = 3.96 × 10⁻¹⁰ m.

**22. D.** E = mc², so m = E/c² = (1.80 × 10¹⁴ J)/(2.9979 × 10⁸ m/s)² = 2.00 × 10⁻³ kg (1 J = 1 kg·m²/s²).

**23. D.** A diffraction pattern can be explained only in terms of waves, so electron diffraction confirmed that electrons have an associated wavelength, λ = h/mv.

**24. C.** Waves in phase (peaks matching) add constructively to give bright areas; waves out of phase (peak meets trough) cancel to give dark spots.

**25. C.** Matter and energy are not distinct. Large pieces of matter behave mainly as particles, photons mainly as waves (no rest mass), and electrons clearly show both.

**26. B.** A line spectrum contains only a few discrete wavelengths, which result from changes between discrete (quantized) energy levels. If any energy were allowed, the spectrum would be continuous.

**27. A.** White light (like a rainbow) contains all wavelengths of visible light, giving a continuous spectrum. Excited atoms give line spectra.

**28. A.** E₃ = −2.178 × 10⁻¹⁸ J (1²/3²) = −2.42 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. The negative sign means the bound electron is lower in energy than at n = ∞ (E = 0).

**29. D.** ΔE = −2.178 × 10⁻¹⁸ J (1/2² − 1/5²) = −4.574 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. λ = hc/|ΔE| = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴)(2.9979 × 10⁸)/(4.574 × 10⁻¹⁹ J) = 4.343 × 10⁻⁷ m = 434 nm (visible, violet).

**30. D.** ΔE = −2.178 × 10⁻¹⁸ J (1/4² − 1/2²) = −2.178 × 10⁻¹⁸ J (0.0625 − 0.2500) = 4.08 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. The positive sign shows energy is absorbed (λ = hc/ΔE = 486 nm).

**31. A.** Removing the electron means n_final = ∞: ΔE = −2.178 × 10⁻¹⁸ J (1/∞ − 1/1²) = 2.178 × 10⁻¹⁸ J per atom. Per mole: (2.178 × 10⁻¹⁸ J)(6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹) = 1.312 × 10⁶ J/mol = 1312 kJ/mol.

**32. A.** n_initial = 3, n_final = ∞: ΔE = −2.178 × 10⁻¹⁸ J (0 − 1/3²) = 2.42 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.

**33. B.** For He⁺, Z = 2: E₁ = −2.178 × 10⁻¹⁸ J (2²/1²) = −8.712 × 10⁻¹⁸ J.

**34. C.** ΔE = −2.178 × 10⁻¹⁸ J (3²)(1/2² − 1/3²) = −2.722 × 10⁻¹⁸ J. λ = hc/|ΔE| = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴)(2.9979 × 10⁸)/(2.722 × 10⁻¹⁸ J) = 7.296 × 10⁻⁸ m = 73.0 nm. (Forgetting the Z² = 9 factor gives a wavelength 9 times too long, 657 nm.)

**35. B.** ΔE = E_final − E_initial < 0 means the atom lost energy and is now more stable; a photon is emitted. Use |ΔE| in λ = hc/ΔE, since a negative wavelength is meaningless.

**36. B.** Bohr's model reproduced hydrogen's energy levels but did not work for other atoms. It was historically important, but modern theory is not derived from it, and electrons do not move in circular orbits.

**37. C.** The visible lines of hydrogen come from electrons falling from higher levels to n = 2. Transitions to n = 1 release more energy and lie in the ultraviolet.
