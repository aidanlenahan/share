Formal charge (bond-breaking view)\wd\The charge on an atom in a molecule if each electron-pair bond is hypothetically broken homolytically, so that each bonding partner receives one electron; it assumes equal sharing of bonding electrons.\ww\Homolytic bond cleavage\wd\Breaking a covalent bond so that each partner receives one of the two bonding electrons; used to assign formal charges.\ww\Heterolytic bond cleavage\wd\Breaking a covalent bond so that both bonding electrons go to one partner, typically the more electronegative element (giving it an octet); used to assign oxidation numbers.\ww\Oxidation number (oxidation state)\wd\The charge an atom would have if every bond were broken heterolytically, with the bonding pair given to the more electronegative partner; it assumes the bonding is 100% ionic (Zumdahl p 113).\ww\Formal charge vs oxidation number\wd\Two extremes of assigning bonding electrons: formal charge assumes equal (covalent) sharing, oxidation number assumes 100% ionic transfer. Neither accurately describes heteronuclear diatomics; the actual calculated charge is intermediate.\ww\Oxidation number rule 1 (sum)\wd\The oxidation numbers of all atoms must add up to the overall charge on the molecule or ion: Σ(oxidation numbers) = charge.\ww\Oxidation number rule 2 (elements)\wd\An atom in its elemental form (e.g. O₂, S₈, Fe, Cl₂) always has an oxidation number of zero.\ww\Oxidation number rule 3 (nonmetals)\wd\Nonmetals usually have negative oxidation numbers.\ww\Oxidation number of fluorine\wd\Fluorine is always −1 in compounds (it is the most electronegative element).\ww\Oxidation number of the other halogens\wd\Cl, Br and I are −1 in most compounds (exceptions occur, e.g. when bonded to O or F).\ww\Oxidation number of oxygen\wd\Usually −2, except when combined with fluorine (e.g. OF₂, where O is +2) or when there is an O–O bond (peroxides).\ww\Oxidation number of O in H₂O₂\wd\−1, because of the O–O bond: 2(+1) + 2x = 0 gives x = −1.\ww\Oxidation number of hydrogen\wd\+1 when bonded to nonmetals; Using CN rather than counting bonding pairs handles multiple bonds correctly.\ww\VSEPR notation AXₓEₑ\wd\A = central atom, X = bonded atoms (x = CN), E = lone pairs (e = LP); x + e = steric number. Hotter bodies peak at shorter wavelength, e.g. λmax at 1000 K is shorter than at 800 K.\ww\Oscillator model of black-body radiation\wd\Radiation of a given frequency was assumed to come from charged particles (oscillators: electrons as 'springs' on atoms as 'vibrating balls') at the surface undergoing simple harmonic motion at that frequency; many oscillators give many frequencies (white light at high T).
