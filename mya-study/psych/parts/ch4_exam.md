---

## Answer Key

**1. B.** The Greeks and Callatians had opposite customs (dialect) but shared the same duty to honor their parents (grammar). Evolution builds a shared toolkit, not fixed identical behaviors, so D is wrong.

**2. D.** The guide defines evolutionary psychology as focusing mostly on what makes humans alike. Explaining individual differences or within-lifetime change belongs to experience and culture.

**3. A.** This is the guide's definition. B describes inheritance of acquired traits, which is not natural selection, and C describes epigenetics.

**4. A.** A mutation is a random error in gene replication and is one source of variation. C is wrong because new gene combinations at conception are another source.

**5. C.** More babyish faces activated the nucleus accumbens more strongly, even in women who had never had children. Because the effect did not depend on being a mother, A is wrong.

**6. C.** Strong social ties meant being 50% more likely to be alive about 7.5 years later, an effect comparable to quitting smoking. This is the key evidence for the need to belong.

**7. A.** Cacioppo argues loneliness evolved as a warning signal, like hunger, because isolation could mean death for our ancestors.

**8. D.** Immunity is lowest in the first trimester, which is when a disease-avoidance system should be most active. That is the logic behind treating disgust as disease avoidance.

**9. B.** The foxes were selected only for tameness, so the floppy ears, curly tails, and piebald coats came along unselected. A is wrong because appearance was never selected.

**10. A.** Separate groups facing the same pressure evolved the same trait through different genetic routes, which is the definition of convergent adaptation.

**11. D.** Herding, a cultural practice, changed the environment, and that environment selected the genes. A describes expression, but the milk example is selection, not expression.

**12. C.** In the clear box, chimps skipped the visibly useless poke while children kept copying it, even when they thought no one was watching.

**13. B.** In the opaque box, copying both steps is sensible for anyone. Only a visibly useless step reveals faithful copying (overimitation).

**14. D.** Overimitation looks inefficient, but it lets skills, rituals, and traditions pass on even when no one understands every step, which makes it a foundation of culture.

**15. A.** Sweet and fat cravings were adaptive when those foods were scarce and are mismatched now that they are everywhere. The other options are peer influence, cultural perception, and the domestication package.

**16. B.** Editing kept the fear reaction identical, so only the object differed. Any difference in learning must then come from the object itself.

**17. C.** The monkeys learned to fear the snake and crocodile but not the flowers or rabbit, so they were not just copying any fear they saw. They were prepared to learn fears of ancestral dangers.

**18. A.** Mikhail proposed shared, unconscious, intuitive principles, such as intended harm versus side effect, applied even when people cannot explain them. B is wrong because cultures weigh moral principles differently.

**19. D.** The seven are helping family, helping your group, returning favors, being brave, deferring to superiors, dividing resources fairly, and respecting others' property. Forgiving enemies is not on the list.

**20. B.** All seven were judged good wherever they were observed, and none was treated as bad, but cultures weigh them differently. A contradicts the finding.

**21. C.** Holding the outcome constant means that only the manner of death varies, so any difference in judgment must come from that.

**22. D.** In Switch the death is a side effect, and in Loop it is a means. Personal force (A) is what separates Loop from Footbridge.

**23. A.** Both Loop and Footbridge use the death as a means, and only Footbridge adds personal force.

**24. C.** This is the Loop dilemma: his body is the brake (a means), but pulling a lever involves no personal force.

**25. B.** The averages were Switch 81%, Loop 72%, Footbridge 51%, with approval falling as harm becomes a means and then adds personal force.

**26. A.** The ordering Switch > Loop > Footbridge held in all 42 countries, which is the grammar. The level varied, which is the dialect.

**27. D.** Relational mobility describes a society, not an individual. It is high in the Americas, Europe, and Australia/New Zealand and low in East Asia and the Middle East, so B is wrong.

**28. B.** In low-mobility societies you cannot escape your reputation, so endorsing pushing could mark you as cold or dangerous.

**29. C.** After accounting for non-independence of neighboring countries, relational mobility predicted only Footbridge judgments. The universal ordering was not challenged, so A is wrong.

**30. D.** The sample was self-selected online, about 75% men, 75% under 32, and 73% college-educated. The study also measured hypothetical judgments, not behavior, so C is wrong.
